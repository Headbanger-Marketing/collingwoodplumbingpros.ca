Plan the request

Explaining what was changed during do-it-yourself work

Work done by an owner can be hard for a later provider to read. A joint, a fitting or a replaced part may look sound yet differ from what the rest of the system uses. For your Collingwood home, list what was changed, roughly when, which parts and joining methods were used, and where receipts, packaging or before and after photos are kept. Say plainly if you are unsure of any detail.

  • What was changed

    Name each fixture, line or device that was altered, and say whether the work was a replacement, an addition or a removal.

  • Parts and joining methods

    List the parts used and how they were joined, and keep any receipts or packaging that show the product names.

  • Photos and dates

    Gather before and after photos and note roughly when the work happened and who in the household carried it out.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before You Send a Request

A few clear photos help a provider understand the job before the first call. Have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the rating plate, the vent pipe where it leaves the tank, and the rental sticker or contract if there is one.
  • Blocked drain: which fixtures back up, whether it only happens on the lowest floor, and the floor drain or cleanout cap if you can find it.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the line beside the water meter. Copper is orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey with threaded joints, and lead is dull grey and shows bright silver when scratched lightly with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Collingwood Request

Should I mention plumbing changes made by someone in my household in Collingwood?

Yes. Say what was changed, roughly when, which parts were used and how they were joined, even if the work seemed minor. A provider can only judge what to look at when the account is accurate, and an incomplete account may send the visit in the wrong direction. Nobody is asking you to defend the work.

What records help when I describe earlier owner work on a Collingwood house?

Receipts, packaging, model numbers, and photos taken before and after are the most useful, along with a rough date and a note of who did the work. If none exist, say so plainly and describe what you remember. A short, plain list is more useful than a confident guess.

After I send an account of my own plumbing changes, is a provider lined up for my Collingwood address?

No. Sending a request only passes your description to our enquiry team for review. Coverage of the exact address, availability, qualifications, written scope, pricing and scheduling must each be confirmed by a provider before any work is agreed, and until then nothing has been booked.

Can a sump pump I replaced myself and a new stain go in one request?

Yes, when the symptoms may be connected. List each change and each symptom as its own line, with dates, so the order of events is clear. Unrelated items, such as a tap swap in one room and a softener question in another, read more clearly as separate requests.

What if I cannot remember what was changed in my Collingwood plumbing?

Say so, and describe what you can see: where a part looks different from the pipes beside it, any tape, clamps or mismatched fittings, and any markings on parts. Photos of those points give a provider something to work from. Do not take anything apart to find out; the respondent will establish the rest.

Will the provider judge or criticize earlier owner work on my plumbing?

This site passes no judgement on owner work and asks only for an accurate account. What a provider concludes, and whether a permit or inspection applies to the proposed work, is for the respondent and the municipality to settle with you. Describe the facts as you know them and leave the assessment to them.
